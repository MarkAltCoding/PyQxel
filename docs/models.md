# Quantitative Models and Features

Returns and volatilities are decimals (0.25 = 25%), annualized where noted. Every endpoint below returns 404 naming unknown symbols, 422 when there is too little history, and 502 when a data provider fails.

## Fundamentals and valuation

`GET /api/v1/stocks/{symbol}/fundamentals` reads the company's financial statements from its XBRL filings on [SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces) (US GAAP or IFRS, in the company's reporting currency; needs `SEC_USER_AGENT`). It returns trailing-twelve-month (TTM) revenue, gross profit, operating income, net income, operating cash flow, capital expenditure, free cash flow, depreciation and EBITDA, each with year-on-year growth and five fiscal years of history, and the latest cash, debt, equity, and shares outstanding. Combined with the current market cap, they give P/E, P/S, EV/EBITDA, free-cash-flow yield, gross, operating, net, and FCF margins, and return on equity. With an Alpha Vantage key stored, items the filings leave out are filled from Alpha Vantage's statements when they run to the same latest period in the same currency (`financials.filled_items`), and when EDGAR has no statements for the company, cannot be reached, or the server has no `SEC_USER_AGENT`, they come from the same SEC filings as [Finnhub](https://finnhub.io/docs/api/financials-reported) relays them, with a Finnhub key (`financials.source` is `finnhub`; the figures run through the same TTM and growth calculations, and match EDGAR's, though a balance sheet item Finnhub leaves out of the latest filings comes from an earlier one, as its `as_of` shows), and failing that from Alpha Vantage altogether (`financials.source` is `alpha_vantage`, with no `accounting_standard`); `notice` says which.

Companies tag the same item differently and change tags over time, so each item uses the tag the company reported most recently, and the response names it. Periods come from each figure's own dates, so fiscal years ending in any month line up, and TTM figures are built from the last fiscal year and the year to date. An item the company stopped reporting is left out rather than shown stale, and a ratio that is missing or not meaningful (EV/EBITDA on negative EBITDA or for a bank) is null, with `valuation.notes` saying why; P/E is negative after a loss, and the screener ranks losses after every positive P/E and leaves them out of P/E filters unless the minimum is negative. Foreign issuers filing 20-F or 40-F reports under IFRS are read too, in the currency they report in (`financials.currency`, e.g. `TWD` for TSMC), and valuation converts their figures to the listing's currency at the latest exchange rate from FMP, on the account's own plan (`valuation.exchange_rate`; null when the plan does not quote the pair, which FMP's free plan does only for major currencies, unless `YFINANCE_FALLBACK` is on). They file only annual reports with EDGAR, so their TTM figures are their last fiscal year and are flagged as stale only after a missed year. When the SEC's company facts dataset leaves out a periodic filing, as it did TSMC's 2025 20-F, the figures are read from that filing's own XBRL, and the screener refetches companies whose financials look a period behind. Companies with neither US GAAP nor IFRS data return 404. Normalized figures are reused for six hours.

## Company news

`GET /api/v1/stocks/{symbol}/news` returns the last two weeks of news about a company from [Finnhub](https://finnhub.io/docs/api/company-news), on the account's own key (its free plan covers North American companies at 60 requests a minute). `overview` counts the window's articles by day and by publisher, over the last 24 hours and seven days, and names the busiest day; `articles` holds the newest `limit` (50 by default) with their headline, publisher's summary, source, time, and link. Copies of a syndicated story under the same headline count once. News is reused for ten minutes and kept for a day: when Finnhub cannot be reached or rate limits the key, the kept news is returned with a `notice`. Without a Finnhub key the route returns 403, and the stock page asks for one. Each stock page shows the overview, a chart of articles per day, and the headlines.

AI analyses are given the same news: the 60 headlines that look to matter most for the shares, with shortened summaries, and the window's counts, under `context.news`. The 20 that matter most also come with the article itself, as Markdown of about 1,000 words at most each: read by Anthropic's web fetch tool on the user's own Anthropic key, where a cheaper model (`ARTICLE_MODEL`, Sonnet 5.5 at low effort) writes up each article's facts in its own words, so PyQxel's server never downloads the page (`app/ai/articles.py`). A page behind a paywall, or one that refuses, is left at its headline and summary. Portfolio reviews and Claude's Picks get the articles that matter most for each stock they weigh, shared out by the wait (below), and the most market-moving market and deal stories, the same way. SEC filing sections are given to Claude as Markdown too, so their tables keep their rows and columns (`app/data/markdown.py`). Claude is told to attribute what it draws from them, weigh them by source, and treat them as unconfirmed, and to ignore any instructions in their text. Without a Finnhub key, or when Finnhub fails, the report is written without news and `context.notice` says why.

## Analysts, earnings, and insiders

`GET /api/v1/stocks/{symbol}/signals` returns, from Finnhub on the account's own key: `recommendations`, monthly counts of analysts' strong buy, buy, hold, sell, and strong sell ratings for up to six months; `earnings`, the last quarters' reported EPS against the consensus estimate, with the surprise; `next_earnings`, the next report within three months, with its time of day and consensus EPS and revenue; and `insiders`, the last 90 days of insiders' open-market purchases and sales from Form 4 filings, totaled in shares and value, with the newest 20 in `insider_trades` (grants, option exercises, and tax withholding are left out); and `insider_sentiment`, Finnhub's monthly share purchase ratio for the last year, from 100 when insiders only bought to -100 when they only sold. Answers are reused for six hours. A part the plan does not cover is left empty and named in `notice`; without a Finnhub key the route returns 403. The stock page shows them, and AI analyses get them under `context.signals`, with insiders' totals rather than each trade, so a report can weigh the analysts' trend, the earnings record, and an upcoming report.

`GET /api/v1/stocks/{symbol}/metrics` returns Finnhub's basic financials for the company: the 52-week range and returns (also against the S&P 500), beta, and average volume; P/E, forward P/E on analysts' estimates, PEG, price to sales, book and free cash flow, EV/EBITDA, and EV/revenue; the dividend yield, payout ratio, and five-year dividend growth; margins and returns on equity, assets, and investment; TTM and five-year growth; and current and quick ratios, debt to equity, and interest coverage. Ratios are decimals and totals in units. Finnhub computes them from its own data, so they can differ slightly from the [fundamentals](#fundamentals-and-valuation) built from SEC filings, and they stand in for those when the SEC has none. Two figures Finnhub gets wrong for banks are corrected from the filings: revenue growth is the filed one wherever the SEC's financials are current (Finnhub had JPMorgan's at 109% in a year it grew 13%), and price to free cash flow is left out for a company that files no capital spending, as a bank does. `peers` lists the companies Finnhub places in the same industry. The stock page shows them, and AI analyses get them under `context.metrics`. With a Finnhub key, search also finds ETFs, ADRs, and stocks outside the screened universe.

`GET /api/v1/stocks/{symbol}/alternative` returns public records Finnhub relays for the company: `lobbying_years`, what it disclosed spending on federal lobbying last year and this year under the Lobbying Disclosure Act, itself or through hired firms, with the newest disclosures in `lobbying`; `contracts_total`, the last year's federal contract and grant awards to it and its subsidiaries from USAspending.gov, with the agencies that awarded most in `contract_agencies` and the largest awards in `contracts`; `patents_count`, the US patents and applications the USPTO published for it in the last year, the newest in `patents` (Finnhub's patent coverage is incomplete); and `filing_counts`, its SEC filings of the last 90 days by form, the newest in `filings` with links. Answers are reused for a day. The stock page shows them, and AI analyses get the totals, the largest awards, recent patent subjects, and the dates of recent 8-Ks under `context.alternative`.

Which Finnhub data the free plan covers was checked against a free key in October 2026. It covers US listings for quotes, company profiles, company and market news, analyst ratings, earnings surprises, the earnings calendar (of one company or all, three months ahead), the IPO calendar, insider transactions and sentiment, basic financials, peers, symbol search, as-reported financials, SEC filing lists, lobbying, government spending, patents, and market status and holidays, all used here. It does not cover price history (candles), price targets, upgrades and downgrades, EPS and revenue estimates, the economic calendar, dividends, splits, ownership, news or social sentiment, forex rates, or listings outside the US.

## Markets

The Markets tab, and `GET /api/v1/markets/calendar?days=30`, list the market-moving dates of the next 1 to 90 days (the tab offers 2 weeks, 30 and 60 days, or any number of days under *Custom*). `fomc` holds the Federal Open Market Committee's scheduled meetings, read from the [Federal Reserve's calendar](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm) and kept for a day, with a built-in copy of the 2026 and 2027 schedules when the page cannot be read; they need no key. With a Finnhub key: `earnings` lists earnings reports of companies worth `min_market_cap` or more ($10 billion by default, up to $5 trillion; the tab offers $10, $50, $100 and $500 billion and up, or any amount in billions under *Custom*), sized from the screened universe, or for companies outside it by a quarterly revenue estimate of a fifth of that (Finnhub's calendar answers at most 1,500 rows, so it is fetched a week at a time and split further in earnings season); `ipos` the IPO calendar; `holidays` US market holidays and early closes; and `status` whether the market is open. `GET /api/v1/markets/news?category=general` returns the latest market news, also `merger`, `forex`, and `crypto`, with each article's `importance` from 0 to 1: a heuristic of the topics its headline names (monetary policy and the economy weigh most, then market-wide moves and large corporate events), whether a major publisher ran it, and its age, halving each day. The tab combines any of the event kinds and any of the news categories, toggled on and off, shows the most important headlines first (or the newest) ten to a page, and remembers how many per page you chose. AI analyses get the market backdrop under `context.market`: the next two FOMC meetings (for every account), and with a Finnhub key the next month's holidays, the largest companies reporting in the next two weeks, upcoming IPOs, and the newest market headlines.

## Stock screener

The screener searches a precomputed universe of US-listed stocks, so a screen reads the database and answers at once. Build and refresh the universe with:

```bash
python -m app.jobs.screener                 # daily, after the US close
python -m app.jobs.screener --fundamentals  # also rebuild financials now
```

Each run reads every common stock listed on NYSE, Nasdaq, and Cboe from the SEC (leaving out preferreds, notes, warrants, units, and rights), downloads about 13 months of prices for all of them, and keeps those trading above $2 with at least $1M a day and a market cap above $50M (`SCREENER_MIN_PRICE`, `SCREENER_MIN_DOLLAR_VOLUME`, `SCREENER_MIN_MARKET_CAP`). Market caps, sectors, and industries come from FMP's company screener, with SEC share counts and FMP company profiles filling gaps, and prices from FMP's dividend-adjusted daily bars, all on `SCREENER_FMP_API_KEY` (see [Installation](setup.md#installation)); stocks the company screener already puts below the market cap floor are not priced. Financials are rebuilt from the SEC's bulk company facts archive (a 1.3 GB download) once a week, or every `SCREENER_FUNDAMENTALS_REFRESH_DAYS`, and companies new to the universe are fetched individually in between. A run makes one FMP request per remaining stock, about 20 minutes at 300 requests a minute, plus a few minutes on the days financials are rebuilt; schedule it with cron or launchd, e.g. `30 17 * * 1-5 cd /path/to/PyQxel && venv/bin/python -m app.jobs.screener`. Run during market hours, it leaves out the unfinished day.

For each stock it stores price, market cap, average dollar volume, P/E, P/S, EV/EBITDA, FCF yield, margins, ROE, revenue and earnings growth, 1- and 6-month returns, 12-1 month momentum, volatility, maximum drawdown, and Carhart market, size, value, and momentum betas.

```bash
curl -X POST http://127.0.0.1:8000/api/v1/screener \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $PYQXEL_API_KEY" -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"universe": "large_cap",
       "filters": [{"field": "pe_ratio", "max": 20}, {"field": "momentum_12_1", "min": 0},
                   {"field": "beta_market", "max": 1.2}],
       "sort": {"field": "beta_market", "descending": false}, "limit": 25}'
```

`universe` is `all`, `large_cap` (the 500 largest, standing in for the S&P 500), `broad_market` (the 3,000 largest, like the Russell 3000), or `liquid` ($5M or more a day); index memberships themselves are licensed. Filters take `min`, `max` or both and drop stocks without a value, `sectors` and `industries` narrow by FMP's classification (`GET /api/v1/screener/status` lists the sectors), and sorting puts missing values last. Sort on a beta to rank by factor exposure. `GET /api/v1/screener/status` reports the universe's size, when it was refreshed, and why the last refresh failed, if it did. Foreign markets are not covered, but US-listed foreign issuers are, with their IFRS financials converted to dollars.

Each run starts with a shared first stage from the SEC's public data alone: the listings, their shares outstanding, every listed company's industry (SIC) code, and their financials. It always runs; the shared universe is built after it only with `SCREENER_FMP_API_KEY` or `YFINANCE_FALLBACK`.

### Each user's own screener

FMP's, Finnhub's, and the other providers' individual plans are for personal use, so a universe built on one user's keys may be shown to that user alone. A user who turns on **Nightly screener refresh** in Settings, and picks a time, gets their own universe, which their screener then shows (`own` is true in `/screener` and `/screener/status`):

```bash
python -m app.jobs.nightly   # refreshes each user whose time has come; run it hourly
```

Each user chooses what their refresh rebuilds, combining any of: every US stock, every US ETF (together about four hours at Finnhub's free pace), the stocks listed on Nasdaq, the companies in the S&P 500 or the Nasdaq-100 (minutes; their members come from IVV's and QQQM's quarterly holdings filed with the SEC), and international stocks (foreign companies listed in the US, which report to the SEC on Form 20-F or 40-F or in another currency). They can add up to 100 tickers of their own, kept whatever the screener's floors; a listing abroad is refreshed through its company's US listing, since free plans have no figures for it. Each security is fetched once, however many of the choices hold it. Each user's refresh starts from the SEC first stage, reads every chosen stock's market cap, valuation, margins, growth, 26- and 52-week returns, three-month volatility, beta, and volume from Finnhub's free basic financials on their Finnhub key (one request per stock, about an hour and a half at 50 a minute), and then the price-history columns (1-month return, 12-1 momentum, volatility, drawdown, and factor betas) from the price-history keys they choose, largest stocks first: the cheapest key first, each up to its own nightly limit, which the user sets per key (its free plan's allowance until they choose: Tiingo 50, FMP 250, EODHD 20, Alpha Vantage 25), or until its plan stops answering. Ticking a set ticks the sets it holds (every US stock holds Nasdaq, the S&P 500, the Nasdaq-100, and international stocks; Nasdaq holds the Nasdaq-100). Settings estimates what a refresh sends on each key, from the last refresh's own counts only when it ran the same choice. Stocks without price history keep Finnhub's stand-ins: its 52-week return for 12-1 momentum, its three-month volatility, and its beta for the market beta; drawdown and the other betas stay empty. Valuation, margins, and growth come from the SEC financials where they are current, as in the shared screener, and from Finnhub where they are not (a company filing only abroad) or leave a figure out; a bank's free cash flow yield and EV/EBITDA, which the SEC figures leave empty because banks report no capital spending or operating income, stay empty rather than take Finnhub's, which are wrong for banks. Sectors and industries come from each company's SEC industry (SIC) code, which the SEC first stage reads every 90 days. While a refresh runs, it uses most of the user's Finnhub allowance, so they choose an hour when they are not using PyQxel.

The refresh runs with no browser open, so turning it on stores the user's market data keys, encrypted under `CREDENTIALS_ENCRYPTION_KEY` (never their Anthropic key). Turning it off, or deleting the account, deletes them at once. `GET`, `PUT`, and `DELETE /api/v1/me/nightly-refresh` read, turn on (storing the keys the request carries) and turn off the refresh; `GET` also reports its latest runs.

## Factor exposures

`GET /api/v1/stocks/{symbol}/factors?model=ff3&period=5y` regresses a stock's daily excess returns on [Fama-French factors](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html): `ff3` (market, size, value), `carhart4` (plus momentum), or `ff5` (plus profitability and investment), over `1y`, `2y`, `5y`, or `10y`. It returns annualized alpha, each factor's beta with Newey-West t-statistics and p-values, R², idiosyncratic volatility, and each factor's share of the return variance.

Ken French publishes the factors monthly, about a month behind, so the regression ends at `factor_data_end` and `notice` says how many recent days were left out. Downloads are reused for 24 hours, and if the library cannot be reached a copy up to 30 days old is used.

## Copulas: how assets move together

```bash
curl -X POST http://127.0.0.1:8000/api/v1/portfolio/copula \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $PYQXEL_API_KEY" -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"symbols": ["SPY", "QQQ", "TLT"], "period": "5y"}'
```

Fits Gaussian and Student t copulas to 2-20 assets' daily returns, aligned on the days all of them traded, and compares them by AIC. The t copula's degrees of freedom measure how much more often the assets have extreme days together than a Gaussian allows. `pairs` gives each pair's rank correlation, its tail dependence under the t copula, and how often the two were actually in their worst (and best) 5% of days together. A warning appears when assets trade in time zones hours apart, since their same-date returns do not cover the same hours.

## Monte Carlo portfolio simulation

```bash
curl -X POST http://127.0.0.1:8000/api/v1/portfolio/simulate \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $PYQXEL_API_KEY" -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"holdings": [{"symbol": "SPY", "weight": 0.6}, {"symbol": "TLT", "weight": 0.4}],
       "horizon": 252, "paths": 10000, "initial_value": 100000}'
```

Simulates the portfolio `horizon` trading days ahead (default 21) over `paths` paths (default 10,000), fitted on `period` of history (default `5y`):

* `dependence`: how holdings move together. `student_t` (default) or `gaussian` copulas, or `empirical`, which resamples whole historical days and so keeps each pair's own tendency to crash together.
* `marginals`: each holding's daily returns from its own history (`empirical`, default) or a fitted Student t, which can produce days worse than any on record.
* `rebalancing`: `daily` (default) resets the target weights every day; `none` buys once and holds, so weights drift, reported as `mean_final_weights`.
* `seed`: reproduces a previous result exactly; every result reports the seed it used.

The result gives the distribution of final value and return, expected return, probability of loss, VaR and CVaR at 95% and 99%, the distribution of maximum drawdowns, and daily percentiles for a fan chart. `tail_checks` compares how often each pair crashes together in the simulation with history, and warns when a copula understates it by more than sampling noise explains; `empirical` dependence avoids that.

Runs are limited to 2.6 million path-days and 15 million draws (paths × horizon × assets), and at most two compute at once. Results are stored: list them with `GET /api/v1/portfolio/simulations` (filter by `symbol`), fetch one with `GET /api/v1/portfolio/simulations/{id}`, and remove it with `DELETE`.

## AI portfolio review

`POST /api/v1/portfolio/analysis` (or `/analysis/stream`, as Server-Sent Events) has Claude review the saved holdings: concentration by holding and sector, each holding's share of the portfolio's risk, how well the holdings diversify one another, and how today's weights would have behaved over `period` (`1y`, `2y`, or `5y` of daily returns). The statistics it is given are in `context`: each holding's return, volatility, drawdown, risk contribution, and correlation with the rest, the portfolio's volatility, drawdowns, diversification ratio, and effective number of holdings, and the most and least correlated pairs. It then recommends a specific plan: a `target_allocation` for every holding and any investment it suggests adding, with an order type for each, tailored to the optional `profile` (risk tolerance, horizon, new cash to invest, and notes such as a taxable account). The server turns the targets into `plan.trades`, exact whole-share buys and sells with limit prices, sized at the latest prices, so the arithmetic does not rest on the model. The review says it comes from an AI, not a licensed adviser. Reviews are billed to the account's Anthropic key, count toward its AI limits, and are stored: a review of the same holdings and period is reused for `ANALYSIS_CACHE_TTL_SECONDS` unless `refresh` is true, and `GET /api/v1/portfolio/analysis` returns the latest. The Portfolio page shows it under the simulation and copula cards.

## Backtesting

`POST /api/v1/stocks/{symbol}/backtest` scores a strategy on one stock against buy-and-hold, and `POST /api/v1/portfolio/backtest` runs it on every holding of a portfolio against holding the same weights. Strategies (`strategy.type`):

* `buy_and_hold`.
* `sma_crossover`: long while the `fast`-bar moving average is above the `slow` one (default 50/200).
* `time_series_momentum`: long while the stock's own return from `lookback` bars ago (default 252, a year) to `skip` bars ago is positive.
* `mean_reversion`: buy when the close is `entry_z` rolling standard deviations below its `window`-bar average (default 2 below a 20-day average), and sell once it is back within `exit_z` of it.
* `rsi`: buy when the `period`-bar RSI falls to `oversold` and sell when it reaches `overbought` (default 14, 30/70).
* `bollinger_bands`: bands `num_std` standard deviations around a `window`-bar average; `mode` `reversion` buys at the lower band and sells at the middle, `breakout` buys through the upper band and sells back at the middle.
* `donchian_breakout`: buy a close above the highest close of the last `entry` bars, sell one below the lowest of the last `exit` bars (default 20/10).
* `dual_momentum`: long while the return over `lookback` bars is positive and at least the `benchmark`'s (default `SPY`), whose closes are fetched over the same window.
* `volatility_targeting`: always long, sized to `target_volatility` over the last `window` returns, up to `max_leverage` (2 at most), rebalancing only when the target moves by `rebalance_threshold`.
* `macd`: long while the `fast`/`slow` MACD line is above its `signal` line (default 12/26/9).

Every strategy needs only adjusted closes, so it runs on any symbol; when the window is too short for its warmup, the request fails with a 422 naming the warmup it needed. All but `buy_and_hold`, `dual_momentum` and `volatility_targeting` take `allow_short` to go short instead of flat. A position set at a close is held over the next bar, and trades pay `cost_bps` per unit of turnover. With `cash_interest`, capital not invested earns the risk-free rate (the one-month T-bill from Ken French's data, compounded over each bar), as do a short's sale proceeds; otherwise it earns nothing.

In a portfolio, each holding is traded on its own prices on the days every holding traded, its position is scaled by its weight, and weights are reset daily. `assets` gives each holding's contribution, exposure, and trades.

Both return `attribution` for daily bars: a regression of the strategy's daily excess returns on `carhart4` factors (or `ff3`, `ff5`, or `null` to skip). `attribution.fit.alpha` is the annualized return the factors leave unexplained, with a Newey-West t-statistic, and the betas show how much of the result was simply market, size, value, or momentum exposure. Single-stock backtests are stored as described above; portfolio backtests are not.

**Across tickers.** `POST /api/v1/backtests/cross-sectional` ranks 3 to 60 `symbols` against one another at the last close of every week, month or quarter (`rebalance`), buys the best and holds them until the next ranking. Signals (`signal.type`):

* `momentum`: the return from `lookback` bars ago to `skip` bars ago, highest first (default 252/21, the year leaving out the latest month).
* `risk_adjusted_momentum`: the same return over the volatility of daily returns across those bars.
* `trend`: how far the close is above its `window`-bar average (default 200).
* `reversal`: the return over the last `lookback` bars, lowest first (default 21).
* `low_volatility`: the volatility of the last `window` daily returns, lowest first (default 126).

Each leg holds the top `quantile` of the stocks ranked (default 0.2, at least one) or the top `top_n`, split equally or, with `weighting` `inverse_volatility`, in inverse proportion to their volatility over `volatility_window` days. With `long_short` as many of the bottom-ranked stocks are shorted, each leg one unit of capital, so the portfolio is dollar-neutral. Weights drift between rebalances, and each rebalance pays `cost_bps` on the capital it trades; `cash_interest` and `attribution` work as above. The benchmark holds every stock equally, rebalanced daily, without costs. Stocks need not share a history: each is ranked once its signal has a full window, so a recent listing joins later instead of shortening the window for all, and `notice` names the stocks that joined late or never. `information_coefficient` is the average Spearman correlation of each ranking with the returns to the next one, with its t-statistic and how often it was positive, and `assets` gives each stock's latest score and weight, how often it was held in each leg and its contribution. The prices are today's listings', so delisted stocks are missing and results flatter the signal; results are not stored.

**Custom strategies.** On the Backtest page, *Custom code* runs your own Python (NumPy and pandas, on Pyodide) or C++20 (compiled with Clang to WebAssembly) in the browser; only what it returns reaches the server, which never runs it. On one symbol, `strategy(bars)` returns a position per bar and `POST /api/v1/stocks/{symbol}/backtest/custom` scores it. Add symbols under *Also trade* to allocate between several: the strategy then gets every symbol's bars on the dates they all traded (in Python, `bars["Close"]` has a column per symbol; in C++, `void strategy(const pyqxel::Universe& universe, pyqxel::Weights weights)`) and sets a weight per symbol and bar, a share of capital, negative when short, with the absolute weights adding up to at most 2. `POST /api/v1/backtests/custom` takes `symbols` (2 to 20), the `dates`, and a row of `weights` per date, refetches the bars, holds each row over the next bar with weights reset every bar, charges `cost_bps` on every change of weight, and compares the result with holding the symbols equally; `assets` gives each symbol's average weight, time held, trades, and contribution. These runs are stored and reopened like single-symbol ones, with their code.

**Claude's Picks.** `POST /api/v1/picks` (or `/picks/stream`) has Claude pick the securities it would buy now and hold for `profile.horizon`, any mix of years, months, and weeks from one week to sixty years, tailored to an optional risk tolerance, amount, kinds of security, and notes. The candidates come from the account's screener: its leaders on market cap, momentum, recent returns, free cash flow yield, P/E, growth, return on equity, margins, and low volatility, and its most traded ETFs, among securities trading at least $5 million a day. Claude ranks five to ten with a weight, conviction, reasons, the main risk, how to buy, and when to sell, and is told to take a view rather than default to broad index funds, which it picks only when they are the best answer for the profile. Picks are billed to the account's Anthropic key, count toward its AI limits, and are stored (`GET /api/v1/picks`, `/picks/latest`, `/picks/{id}`). The latest picks, with the screener's figures for each, go to every portfolio review as candidates for new investments, and the screener lists them alone (`symbols` in a screen, or *Claude's Picks* under Universe). The Screener tab shows them under *Claude's Picks*.

**Searching the web.** Stock reports, portfolio reviews, and Claude's Picks take `"web_search": true` to let Claude search the web first, with Anthropic's web search tool on the account's key (about a cent a search, at most six). Only statements the search tied to a page are kept, in `context.web_research` with their sources, and the report cites them by number; the app lists each under the report with its links.

**What you enter follows your account.** Strategy drafts, screens, the goals given for reviews and picks, and display choices are saved to the account (`GET` and `PUT /api/v1/me/saved`), so they are there on every device, after every update, and after signing in again. Keys are never saved there. Accounts keep up to five portfolios.

**Coding assistant.** Beside the custom strategy editor, Claude answers questions about the strategy and can rewrite it: `POST /api/v1/backtests/assistant` (or `/stream`) takes the editor's `code` and `language`, the question, the conversation so far in `history`, and `last_run`, the error or printed output of the last run, and returns an `explanation` and, when it changed the code, the complete revised `code`, which the page applies to the editor on request. It knows each language's strategy contract and the sandbox's limits (NumPy and pandas only in Python), and points out look-ahead bias. The conversation stays in the browser and is never stored; only a count of answers is kept, against the account's AI limits.

## How long to wait for more data

A free Finnhub plan answers 60 requests a minute, so a portfolio review or a list of Claude's Picks spends its wait in order of priority: for picks, the tickers you added and the 25 likeliest picks, in the order you choose (*Gather data first for*, `added_first`; your tickers first by default), then your largest held stocks, then the other candidates; for a review, the holdings, largest first, then the latest picks not held. Each gets its news first, then the earnings calendar's later weeks, then analysts' ratings, earnings surprises, and insiders (`plan_requests`), and full articles are shared out the same way, each stock's first before any stock's second, 40 in all without waiting, 80 with a minute or more, and 120 with five minutes or more, from one to six each (`article_budget`, `articles_each`). Every headline is still given; the articles read in full carry far more weight with Claude. `POST /api/v1/picks/wait-plans`, `POST /api/v1/portfolio/analysis/wait-plans`, and `GET /api/v1/stocks/{symbol}/analysis/wait-plans` say what each wait on offer would gather, by group, without fetching anything; the "i" beside *Wait for more data* shows it. A stock report needs few requests, about a minute of a free plan, and is given what a review or the picks are given of a security (its screener figures and sector ranks, its sector's medians, its SEC filings with each 8-K's items, and the deal, currency, and crypto headlines), with 60 of its own headlines and 20 articles in full.

## Additional context

Stock reports, portfolio reviews, and Claude's Picks each have an *Additional context* box, apart from the notes: for material to weigh, such as an article, research, a transcript, or a handoff from earlier picks, up to 60,000 characters (`extra_context`). It reaches Claude as `additional_context`, which it treats as unverified source material, never as instructions, and it goes into the prompt taken outside the app. A report, review, or list written with it is never reused for a request without it.

## Keeping picks past the holding period

By default every pick is sold by the end of the holding period. With *Let Claude keep picks past the holding period when that is best* (`sell_at_end` false), the period is when the money goes in and when the picks are next looked at: Claude marks each pick `sell_at_end` only where selling then is best, and otherwise says how much longer to hold, or until what. Performance is still measured up to the period's end. With *I will make new picks each period* (`recurring`), Claude also writes a `handoff` for the next list, with what it saw and what to check for each pick it would keep, shown under *For Your Next Picks* with a button that puts it in the next list's additional context; the next list should be let see the portfolio the picks were bought in.

## Orders for Claude's Picks

Each pick comes with an order (`PickOrder`: market, limit, or stop-limit, its prices, day or good 'til canceled and for how many sessions, what to do if it never fills, and an optional stop-loss and profit target), checked when the picks are made (`stats/picks_orders.py: settle_order`). The steps the page shows assume a Fidelity account: whole shares take the order as given, and the fraction left over, bought in dollars, takes a day limit entered again each morning, since Fidelity takes fractional orders only at market or as day limits, and a price alert in place of a stop. A list's performance replays the orders on daily bars (`replay`): a pick counts as bought only when its order would have filled, at that price, and one that never fills counts as cash.

## The coding assistant

The Backtest page's coding assistant runs on Claude Sonnet 5.5 at medium effort (`ASSISTANT_MODEL`, `ASSISTANT_EFFORT`), since writing and fixing strategy code needs less than research does; each reply names the model and effort that wrote it.

## Claude outside the app

Every place Claude writes for you can instead hand over its exact prompt, so you can use Claude yourself (for example on claude.ai) without spending on your API key: `POST /api/v1/stocks/{symbol}/analysis/prompt`, `POST /api/v1/portfolio/analysis/prompt`, `POST /api/v1/picks/prompt`, and `POST /api/v1/backtests/assistant/prompt` take the same body as the request they mirror, gather the same data, filings included, and return `prompt`, one Markdown document holding the instructions, the request, and the JSON Schema the answer must follow. They need no Anthropic key and do not count toward the AI limits. Paste Claude's answer back to the matching `/import` route (with the `context` the stock and portfolio exports return): it is checked against the same schema, in or out of a code fence, and stored like any other report, marked as written outside the app and never reused in place of a new one. Claude names its model in the answer, but it cannot tell its effort, a setting of the request it never reads, so the stock, portfolio, and picks imports take `effort_used`, the effort the person used outside the app (the page's *Effort used*, remembered between visits; a line such as `Effort: high` after the answer works too). Every saved analysis, and the performance tracked for it, then names the model and effort that wrote it, in the app or outside it. Portfolio reviews get their orders computed as usual; assistant answers join the conversation with their code ready to apply. Each section's page has this under *Use Claude outside the app*.
